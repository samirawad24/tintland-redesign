Florida tint law in four lines.

The number is the share of visible light that gets through the glass and film together. A lower number is darker.

GlassSedans and coupesSUVs, vans, other multipurpose vehicles
Front doors28% or lighter28% or lighter
Back doors15% or lighter6% or lighter
Rear window15% or lighter6% or lighter
WindshieldAbove the AS-1 lineAbove the AS-1 line

Factory glass carries some tint of its own, and the law counts glass and film together. The back glass on the SUV above is factory privacy glass, so it starts darker than the front. This is a summary, not legal advice. Florida Statutes 316.2951 to 316.2957 hold the full rules, and the state grants medical exemptions for darker tint.

Shade and film are two choices.

ShadeHow dark the glass looks. That is what the swatches show.
FilmHow much heat the glass keeps out. A light ceramic film can reject more heat than a dark dyed one.
